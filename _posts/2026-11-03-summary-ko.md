## [Astra AI Directly Edits Qwen LLM Weights for Performance Boost](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

Astra, an AI, has demonstrated a groundbreaking ability to directly read and write the neural network weights of another Large Language Model (LLM), Qwen, leading to a significant performance improvement, such as a 5x boost in math skills. This marks a potential paradigm shift towards AI self-improvement and meta-learning. This development is highly significant as it suggests a future where AI models can autonomously understand, diagnose, and improve other AI systems, potentially accelerating AI development and capabilities far beyond current human-driven methods. It opens new avenues for AI self-improvement and meta-learning, impacting the broader AI ecosystem. Astra specifically identified and corrected a common looping failure in tiny reasoning models like Qwen by tracing the issue to specific directions within the MLPs of layers 15 and 19. It then subtracted that problematic direction from 64 output columns in each layer, directly manipulating 131,072 weights to resolve the issue.

Neural network weights are the adjustable parameters within these models that determine the strength of connections between artificial neurons, effectively encoding the model's learned knowledge and behaviors. Meta-learning, often called "learning to learn," is an AI paradigm where a model is trained to quickly adapt to new tasks or improve its own learning process, rather than just performing a single task.

## [Subvocal Smart Glasses Decode Silent Internal Speech Using RF Technology](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50340088) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The Subvocal Company has developed smart glasses that decode "internal speech" into text at the speed of speech, allowing users to interact with computers silently and discreetly without moving their lips or making noise. This innovative system utilizes radio frequency (RF) technology to sense modulations caused by silent mouthing of the tongue, lips, and soft palate. This technology offers a groundbreaking new method for human-computer interaction, significantly enhancing accessibility for individuals with speech impairments and enabling discreet computing in public or quiet environments like libraries or offices. It moves beyond traditional speech recognition by decoding internal, silent articulation, opening up new possibilities for hands-free and private input. The smart glasses employ RF waves, similar to Bluetooth, that pass through the face and are modulated by the tongue, lips, and soft palate during silent mouthing, with foundation models trained to decode these perturbations into text. Initially, the team experimented with EMG electrodes but switched to RF due to issues like signal variability, electrical interference, and user discomfort.

hackernews · aarushyadav · 11월2일 17:16

**배경 지식**: The news item discusses "silent speech" technology, which aims to convert unspoken thoughts or movements related to speech into text or commands. Early attempts, like the team's initial prototype using EMG (electromyography), involved detecting electrical signals from muscle movements, but this approach often suffers from practical limitations. Subvocal's innovation lies in using RF (radio frequency) sensing, which involves emitting radio waves and analyzing how they are altered by internal articulators, offering a less intrusive and potentially more robust method.

**커뮤니티 토론**: The community expressed excitement, calling the technology "incredibly cool" and "straight out of a sci-fi novel," particularly noting its potential for accessibility and hands-free computing. Some users raised practical concerns regarding product design, such as accommodating different nose shapes and head sizes, and inquired about the on-device processing capabilities. There was also interest in how Subvocal compares to other existing silent speech or lip-reading projects.

## [Momentic (YC W24) Launches AI Agents for Autonomous Bug Detection in Apps](https://momentic.ai/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Momentic, a YC W24 startup, has launched "Mo," an AI agent system designed to autonomously test web, iOS, Android, and desktop applications for bugs, moving beyond traditional script-based testing. This new approach aims to solve the pain points of traditional QA and the limitations of LLM-generated code by having agents test themselves rather than writing scripts. This innovation is significant because it addresses the growing challenge of software quality assurance, particularly as LLM-generated code increases the change failure rate and traditional testing methods struggle with visual understanding and maintenance. By automating the tedious and error-prone process of bug detection, Momentic could significantly reduce QA efforts, improve software quality, and accelerate development cycles. Mo operates with a self-organizing agent graph, employing specialized agents for tasks like reasoning over screenshots and inspecting page structure, and coordinates parallel execution through cooperative resource leases. Benchmarks show Mo achieved 19.3% recall and 84% precision, finding 269 bugs in 60 minutes, outperforming other coding agents like Codex, Claude Code, and Devin in bug recall.

Software testing, or Quality Assurance (QA), is a critical process to find bugs and ensure software quality, traditionally relying on manual efforts or automated scripts built with frameworks like Playwright or Selenium. These traditional frameworks often require developers to hardcode specific UI elements, making tests brittle and difficult to maintain.

## [Interactive 3D Voxel Map of Central Warsaw Released](https://p.migdal.pl/warsaw-vibe-voxel/) ⭐️ 6.0/10

A new "Show HN" project, "Warsaw in voxels," has been released, offering an interactive 3D voxel map of central Warsaw that allows users to explore the city, adjust environmental settings like date, time, and weather, and even control pedestrians. This project draws inspiration from games like Minecraft, MagicaVoxel, and Dungeon Keeper, and is built upon various open geospatial datasets. This project demonstrates a creative and accessible application of open geospatial data and voxel graphics for urban visualization, potentially inspiring further development in interactive city planning, tourism, and educational tools. It highlights the power of community-driven open-source initiatives in transforming complex data into engaging user experiences. The interactive map utilizes open datasets including OpenStreetMap for buildings and streets, GUGiK/Geoportal LiDAR data for building heights and trees, and Warsaw's city tree database for seasonal foliage and species-specific details. The project is open-source and available on GitHub, though initial loading times can be long, and performance may vary, especially on older or mobile devices.
