## [Microsoft Denies Copilot Infringes NYT Copyright](https://www.theverge.com/policy/990267/microsoft-openai-new-york-times-authors-lawsuit) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In new legal filings, Microsoft claims its Copilot chatbot rarely reproduces substantial content from news articles or books, including those from The New York Times, as part of its defense against copyright infringement lawsuits. The company stated that Copilot rarely reproduces even full sentences, let alone substantive chunks that could substitute for the original work. This legal development is significant as the outcome of the lawsuit between Microsoft/OpenAI and The New York Times could set a precedent for copyright law in the era of generative AI, impacting how AI models are trained and how content creators are compensated. It will shape the future of content creation and intellectual property rights in the AI industry. Microsoft's defense hinges on the claim that Copilot rarely reproduces even full sentences from copyrighted materials, implying that any reproduction is insubstantial and does not substitute for the original work. The company provided 8.2 million Copilot outputs as part of the lawsuit's discovery process to support its claims.

**배경 지식**: Generative AI models, such as Microsoft's Copilot, learn from extensive datasets that frequently include copyrighted content from the internet. This practice has led to numerous lawsuits from content creators and publishers who contend that AI outputs can infringe upon their intellectual property by reproducing or creating derivative works without proper authorization or compensation. The central legal dispute in these cases often concerns whether the use of copyrighted data for training and the generation of similar content falls under fair use or constitutes copyright infringement.
