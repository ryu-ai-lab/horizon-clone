## [QuadRF: Open-Source Device Visualizes RF Signals, Drones, and WiFi Through Walls](https://www.jeffgeerling.com/blog/2026/quadrf-can-spot-drones-and-see-wifi-through-my-wall/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

QuadRF is a new open-source, affordable 4x4 MIMO development kit that enables real-time visualization of RF signals, such as WiFi and drones, even through physical barriers like walls. This device offers a novel way to "see" radio waves, providing practical applications for RF signal detection and analysis. This device is significant as it democratizes advanced RF visualization, making sophisticated capabilities like through-wall detection of drones and WiFi accessible to a wider audience. Its open-source nature fosters innovation and could lead to new applications in security, surveillance, and wireless network analysis. QuadRF is a 4x4 MIMO development kit that utilizes beamforming technology, similar to that found in many Wi-Fi routers, to achieve its visualization capabilities. The complete kit, priced at $499, includes a Raspberry Pi 5, a tripod, a 32GB microSD card, and a power supply, with its open-source UI allowing for user customization.

## [New York City Bans Deceptive Subscription Practices with "Click-to-Cancel" Law](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/10/new-york-city-deceptive-subscriptions-ban) ⭐️ 7.0/10

New York City is set to implement a "click-to-cancel" law, making it the first major U.S. city to ban deceptive subscription practices and require companies to offer cancellation processes as straightforward as signing up. This new regulation, announced by Mayor Mamdani, aims to protect consumers from "dark patterns" in subscription management. This regulation is a significant step for consumer protection, potentially setting a precedent for other major U.S. cities and forcing companies across various industries to re-evaluate their subscription management user interfaces and business practices. It addresses the growing issue of "dark patterns" that make it difficult for users to cancel services, fostering greater transparency and fairness in the digital economy. The "click-to-cancel" law specifically mandates that companies must provide a cancellation process that is as simple and direct as the initial sign-up process, aiming to eliminate "dark patterns" designed to trap users in subscriptions. While similar laws exist in California, New York City's implementation marks a significant expansion of such consumer protections within a major urban center.

hackernews · randycupertino · 7월10일 18:26 · [커뮤니티 토론](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48863464)

**배경 지식**: Deceptive subscription practices, often referred to as "dark patterns," are user interface designs that intentionally trick users into making unintended decisions, such as signing up for recurring charges or making it exceedingly difficult to cancel a service. These practices exploit cognitive biases and often involve hidden fees, confusing navigation, or requiring multiple steps and phone calls to terminate a subscription.

**커뮤니티 토론**: The community generally praises the law's intent to protect consumers from deceptive practices, with some users sharing positive examples of companies like Nintendo that make cancellations easy. However, there is skepticism regarding the "landmark" claim, as California already has similar regulations, and concerns are raised about the law's enforceability, particularly concerning "junk fees" and potential carve-outs for certain industries like restaurants.
