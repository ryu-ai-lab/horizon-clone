## [W3C's 1998 "Cool URIs Don't Change" Document Emphasizes Persistent Web Identifiers](https://www.w3.org/Provider/Style/URI) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The 1998 W3C document "Cool URIs Don't Change" by Tim Berners-Lee established the foundational principle that Uniform Resource Identifiers (URIs) should be stable and persistent to ensure the long-term accessibility of web resources. This principle remains critically relevant today, impacting web architecture, digital preservation, and user experience by preventing "link rot" and ensuring information longevity across the internet. The document emphasizes server-side control over URI-to-file mapping and the importance of designing URIs that are independent of file system structure or content changes. Modern mitigation strategies like 301 redirects and CMS-managed slug renames address this, though neglect and website reorgs still cause URI rot.

hackernews · Klaster_1 · 8월9일 14:32 · [커뮤니티 토론](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49231809)

**배경 지식**: A Uniform Resource Identifier (URI) is a string of characters that identifies a resource on the web, such as a webpage, image, or video. It encompasses both Uniform Resource Locators (URLs), which specify how to access a resource, and Uniform Resource Names (URNs), which identify a resource by name. The "Cool URIs Don't Change" principle advocates for designing these identifiers to be stable over time, preventing broken links and ensuring that web content remains accessible for decades.
