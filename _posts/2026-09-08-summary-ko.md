## [LG Smart TVs Accused of Audio Logging and Network Snooping](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6IFVTcM28KA) ⭐️ 9.0/10

LG Smart TVs are reportedly logging audio even when the screen is off and actively snooping on local network devices, impacting an estimated 216 million units globally. This discovery highlights significant privacy concerns due to the devices' intrusive terms and conditions. This issue is highly significant as it represents a major privacy violation for millions of consumers, raising critical questions about consumer rights, data collection practices, and the ethical implications of IoT devices. It underscores the need for greater transparency and control over personal data in connected home electronics. The reported behavior includes logging audio even when the TV screen is off and actively monitoring other devices on the user's local network. LG's contract terms reportedly require users to obtain consent from anyone whose voice might be captured, placing a significant burden on the consumer.

## [Caltech Launches First AI-Powered Research Math Hackathon](https://mathathonchallenge.com/index.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Caltech Mathathon has been launched as the first hackathon specifically dedicated to research-level mathematics, leveraging AI tools and generating significant interest within the community. This innovative event, organized by Caltech undergraduates, aims to explore the intersection of AI and advanced mathematical research. This initiative is significant as it pioneers a new format for fostering AI-driven mathematical research and education, potentially accelerating discoveries and providing students with practical experience in a rapidly evolving field. It highlights the growing role of AI in complex problem-solving and its potential to democratize access to high-level mathematical exploration. The Mathathon is organized by Caltech undergraduates, not officially representing the university or its departments, and aims to promote responsible AI use. A key point of discussion revolves around whether the hackathon format, with its intense short-term work, is well-suited for the iterative and often long-running nature of LLM-driven mathematical progress.
